# Argentina's 2023 Rent Decontrol: Analysis

The news reports that Argentina "abolished rent price controls in late 2023" and that in the first six months Buenos Aires rental listings "more than doubled" and "real rents fell as supply expanded." Under the wiki's frame the report names the directional content of two load-bearing claims already in the corpus: the Mises rent-ceiling claim and the Rothbard maximum-price-control claim. The stance is **endorse**, in the descriptive sense — the event instantiates a prediction the wiki already encodes.

## The Mises Rent-Ceiling Claim

[[mises-on-rent-ceilings|Mises]] in [[liberalism|Liberalism]] states the rent-ceiling case directly: "When the government fixes a ceiling on residential rents, a housing shortage immediately ensues." The mechanism is not about landlord enrichment; it is that the controlled price withdraws units that would otherwise have been offered, with the queue-and-shortage pattern following immediately. The Argentine repeal is the reverse-direction instance of the same claim: removing the binding ceiling restores the clearing rent, and the marginal supply re-enters the market.

## The Rothbard Maximum-Price Claim

[[rothbard-on-price-controls|Rothbard]] in [[power-and-market|Power and Market]] §3.1 gives the general result: an effective maximum price "creates an artificial shortage of the product, which continues *as long as* the control is in existence — in fact, becomes ever worse as resources continue to shift to other products." Two scope claims are operative for the rental case. First, the shortage is generated by the control, not by underlying demand. Second, "the more 'elastic' the supply, i.e., the more resources will shift out of production, the more aggravated, *ceteris paribus*, the shortage will be." Rental housing on the medium horizon is unusually elastic — landlords can convert to sale, withhold units, or shift capital — so the binding ceiling generates a deepening shortage that the wiki's frame predicts will reverse symmetrically on repeal.

The Buenos Aires listings-doubled figure is the supply-side count: units that had been withdrawn under the ceiling re-entered the market once the political-means override on the rent was lifted. The "real rents fell" figure is the price-side count: the restored clearing price came in below the controlled-then-queue-rationed rent on the relevant horizon. Both move together because they are two readings of the same restored market.

## The Frame That Does the Work

The intervention-as-cumulative-process reading in [[state-power-and-intervention|State Power and Intervention]] supplies the broader context: price controls are not one-off techniques but disturbances that produce further distortions and pressure for more intervention. The endorsement is therefore not a "deregulation works" slogan but the specific Mises–Rothbard prediction that the wiki encodes — a binding ceiling produces a shortage that the political process then proposes to fix with further intervention, while repeal restores the market that the calculation the ceiling suppressed.

The wiki has no Argentina-specific empirical material on post-2023 supply-elasticity timelines; the prediction is purely the theoretical generator. The news supplies the empirical instance the wiki was missing.

## Scope and What the Thesis Does Not Claim

The endorsement is descriptive: the wiki predicts this directional pattern under these conditions, and the news reports those conditions. It is not a claim that all rental markets behave identically on all horizons; the Rothbard passage explicitly bounds the result on supply elasticity. It is not a claim about distributional consequences across tenant cohorts. And it is not an endorsement of the wider Milei programme — only of the specific rent-decontrol prediction the wiki's price-theory corpus encodes.
