# Mises on Capital Consumption

> "Antiliberal policy is a policy of capital consumption. It recommends that the present be more abundantly provided for at the expense of the future."
>
> — Ludwig von Mises, [[liberalism|Liberalism: In the Classical Tradition]], Part I §5.

Mises's formulation does not require a wealth-tax statute to apply. It is a general claim about the structural cost of any policy that redirects capital from accumulation toward present transfer: tariffs, subsidies, price controls, redistributive taxation, soft-budget public ownership. Each looks, locally, like distribution of an existing pie; each, structurally, eats into the savings-and-investment flow that determines next period's pie. The "more abundantly provided for" half of the sentence names the visible political surplus; the "at the expense of the future" half names the invisible cost the politics is built to obscure.

The passage matters here because it gives the Austrian intervention argument its *moral-economic* frame, complementary to the praxeological frame in [[economic-calculation-problem|the calculation problem]] and the historical frame in [[political-means-and-economic-means|Oppenheimer's political means]]. Mises does not appeal to natural rights; he points at a measurable consequence and at the rhetorical structure that hides it. He notes in the same passage that the demagogue "denounces [the liberal] as a hard-hearted enemy of the people" while "setting himself up as a friend of humanity" — the politics of capital consumption arrives, predictably, dressed as compassion. That makes the passage a sharper diagnostic instrument than the slogan "redistribution destroys incentives": the claim is not about incentives but about the *stock*, and the stock is what funds wages, employment, and standards of living in subsequent periods.

[[rothbard-on-wealth-tax|Rothbard]] later gives the capital-consumption claim a specific tax instrument in [[power-and-market|Power and Market]]: a tax on individual wealth as the policy that operates *directly* on the capital stock without even the intermediating step of incentive-distortion.
