# Rothbard on Price Controls

> [[power-and-market|Rothbard]] treats a maximum-price control as a categorical instance of *triangular intervention* — an intervener compelling or forbidding an exchange between two other parties. The chapter does not weigh "did the supply curve shift?" against "did demand respond?" as separable empirical questions. It supplies a categorical prediction: at any effective control price below the market-clearing level, "the market is no longer cleared, and the quantity demanded exceeds the quantity supplied." Repealing the control returns the market to clearance.

## The Passage

> "*FP* is the equilibrium price set by the market. Now, let us assume that the intervener imposes a maximum control price *0C*, above which any sale becomes illegal. At the control price, the market is no longer cleared, and the quantity demanded exceeds the quantity supplied by the amount *AB*. In the ensuing shortage, consumers rush to buy goods that are not available at the price… The chief characteristic of a price maximum is the queue, the endless 'lining up' for goods that are not sufficient to supply the people at the rear of the line."

Rothbard makes the elasticity point explicit in the very next paragraph: "even if the stock of a good is frozen for the foreseeable future, and the supply line is vertical, this artificial shortage will still develop." The more elastic the supply, the worse the shortage — because resources shift out of the controlled line. Under *selective* control of a single product, "the artificial shortage created in the particular line will be even more pronounced, since entrepreneurs and factors can shift to the production and sale of other products." The framing he rejects in the same paragraph is the standard regulatory rationale — *"we must impose controls on this product as long as it is in short supply"* — which he calls an "almost ludicrous error" because "price control *creates* an artificial shortage of the product, which continues *as long as* the control is in existence."

## Where the Mechanism Sits in the Typology

Ch. 2 of [[power-and-market|Power and Market]] introduces the *autistic / binary / triangular* intervention typology. A maximum-price control is the canonical triangular case: the State compels a pair of parties (landlord and tenant; producer and consumer) to refrain from an exchange they would otherwise have made. The same machinery covers minimum prices (which produce an unsold *surplus* rather than a shortage), minimum wages (involuntary unemployment as a labor-services surplus), usury laws (rationed credit), and exchange-rate controls (the silver/gold and dollar-shortage cases). The rent ceiling specifically belongs to the maximum-price branch.

## Relation to Mises

The passage extends and operationalizes the older Misesian point in [[liberalism|Liberalism]] that government price-fixing produces "the disappearance from the market of the goods concerned." Mises supplies the political-economy frame and the Vienna illustration; Rothbard supplies the categorical derivation and the typology that locates the rent-ceiling instance inside the wider [[state-power-and-intervention|intervention-as-cumulative-process]] frame.
