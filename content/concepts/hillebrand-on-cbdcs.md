# Hillebrand on Central Bank Digital Currencies

> "Money can be programmed to expire, forcing spending and preventing saving; this implements negative interest rates without the zero lower bound. Money can be programmed to work only in specified regions… Money can refuse purchase categories, blocking payments for alcohol, tobacco, firearms, or politically disfavored goods at the monetary level. Transactions can require verified identity of both parties, making anonymous exchange impossible… Money can be programmed to activate only when conditions are met: vaccination status, social credit score, tax compliance, political loyalty tests."
>
> — Hillebrand, [[the-praxeology-of-privacy|The Praxeology of Privacy]], Ch. 10 §10.5 "Central Bank Digital Currencies as Total Intervention."

Hillebrand's Ch. 10 analyzes CBDCs through Rothbard's three-part intervention typology — autistic (commands to individuals), binary (state as party to exchange), and triangular (state imposing on third-party exchange) — and argues that the CBDC architecture is the first monetary form to combine all three in a single medium. "CBDCs are programmable money enabling comprehensive surveillance and control, not simple digital versions of existing currency." The load-bearing claim is that programmability is "a design choice, not an inherent feature of digital currency"; a CBDC could replicate cash properties (anonymous, bearer-based, unrestricted), but the designs central banks deploy consistently do not.

Two architectural levers carry the typology. Holding caps and forced-expiry are autistic interventions embedded in the medium itself: the currency refuses to retain value past a quantity threshold or after a time threshold, and no separate enforcement action is required because "the transaction simply fails." Direct central-bank balances at the citizen level are binary interventions because the state becomes the counterparty to every balance, so "every economic act is reported instantly to the monetary authority." Merchant acceptance rules, identity verification, and category-of-spending restrictions are triangular interventions imposed on private exchanges through the monetary medium.

The argument is the [[praxeology-of-privacy|Praxeology of Privacy]] frame applied: surveillance attacks the Observe stage of the actor's adversarial control loop, and CBDC architecture is designed to make the observation automatic and inescapable.
