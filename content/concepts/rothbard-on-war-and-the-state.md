# Rothbard on War and the State

> "In war, State power is pushed to its ultimate, and, under the slogans of 'defense' and 'emergency,' it can impose a tyranny upon the public such as might be openly resisted in time of peace. War thus provides many benefits to a State, and indeed every modern war has brought to the warring peoples a permanent legacy of increased State burdens upon society."
> — [[anatomy-of-the-state|Rothbard, Anatomy of the State]]

Rothbard's claim has two operative parts. First, the **slogan effect**: *"defense"* and *"emergency"* license measures that would be refused in peacetime — taxes, conscription, regulation, secrecy, surveillance — and the increase ratchets. He endorses Bourne's compact formulation, *"war is the health of the State,"* and reads twentieth-century state-growth as evidence that the ratchet is durable, not temporary.

Second, the **fear-of-rivals mechanism**: *"Especially has the State been successful in recent centuries in instilling fear of other State rulers… a war between rulers was converted into a war between peoples, with each people coming to the defense of its rulers in the erroneous belief that the rulers were defending them."* The Ruritania/Walldavia passage in [[anatomy-of-the-state|Anatomy of the State]] names the conversion: an inter-state conflict is re-described as a popular self-defense, and the [[citizenship-and-state-bargaining|citizen]] is enlisted into a quarrel that was not his own. Rothbard reads "collective security" arrangements — modern international law that *"stress[es] maximizing the extent of war through such concepts as 'collective security'"* — as the institutional form of the same conversion at multi-state scale. The point converges with [[tilly-on-protection-rackets|Tilly's racketeer definition]]: if the threat is produced or amplified by the protector, the protection charge is racket revenue.
