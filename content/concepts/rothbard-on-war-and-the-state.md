# Rothbard on War and the State

> The passage from [[anatomy-of-the-state|Anatomy of the State]] treats the defense framing not as an isolated rhetorical move but as the standing technique by which the state expands during wars and locks in the expansion afterward. The companion claim about fabricated external threats — *"the State has been successful in recent centuries in instilling fear of other State rulers"* — supplies the demand side that makes the "defense" slogan land.

## The Defense-and-Emergency Passage

> "In war, State power is pushed to its ultimate, and, under the slogans of 'defense' and 'emergency,' it can impose a tyranny upon the public such as might be openly resisted in time of peace. War thus provides many benefits to a State, and indeed every modern war has brought to the warring peoples a permanent legacy of increased State burdens upon society. War, moreover, provides to a State tempting opportunities for conquest of land areas over which it may exercise its monopoly of force. Randolph Bourne was certainly correct when he wrote that 'war is the health of the State,' but to any particular State a war may spell either health or grave injury."

Rothbard's framing is structural rather than partisan. The "defense" and "emergency" labels are not particular abuses; they are the categories under which extraction and centralization that would be resisted in peacetime become tolerable. The ratchet — *"every modern war has brought to the warring peoples a permanent legacy of increased State burdens"* — is the empirical claim that operationalizes the structural one.

## The Fabricated-Threat Companion

The same essay supplies the demand-side mechanism that makes the defense slogan operative. Rothbard observes that the state *"has been successful in recent centuries in instilling fear of other State rulers"*, that the state *"identify itself with the territory it governed"* so that an attack on Ruritania's rulers is reframed as an attack on Ruritania's people, and that *"a war between rulers was converted into a war between peoples, with each people coming to the defense of its rulers in the erroneous belief that the rulers were defending them."* The mechanism converges directly with the racketeer move named in [[tilly-on-protection-rackets|Tilly's 1985 essay]] — the state both produces and offers a price for the shield against the threat — though Rothbard reaches the conclusion through analytic dissection rather than historical sociology.

## Why It Belongs at the Center of the Frame

Sentence 1 of the passage names the state's preferred operating environment; sentence 2 names the ratchet that makes peace-time scrutiny insufficient. That is why this article sits between [[state-power-and-intervention|State Power and Intervention]] — which treats taxation, conscription, and monopoly law as legalized privilege — and [[war-and-state-formation|War and State Formation]] — which gives the historical-sociology layer behind the same claim. The Bourne sentence is the compact slogan; the wiki's load-bearing claim is the ratchet, not the slogan.
