# Tilly on Protection Rackets

> [[war-making-and-state-making-as-organized-crime|Tilly's 1985 essay]] supplies the wiki's most compact non-libertarian convergence on the state-as-racket framing. The opening sentence is the canonical citation; the body specifies a four-part model of state activity and a definition of "racketeer" that names exactly the move governments use to justify extraction.

## The Canonical Passage

> "If protection rackets represent organised crime at its smoothest, then war making and state making — quintessential protection rackets with the advantage of legitimacy — qualify as our largest examples of organised crime. Without branding all generals and statesmen as murderers or thieves, I want to urge the value of that analogy."

Tilly is explicit that this is an analogy advanced for analytical purchase, and that it is bounded by "the European experience of the past few centuries." He is not a libertarian and does not infer market anarchism from the description.

## The Racketeer Definition

The essay supplies a sharp working definition that allows the analogy to do empirical work: *"Someone who produces both the danger and, at a price, the shield against it is a racketeer."* The category of "legitimate protector" is then reserved for an agent who supplies a shield "but has little control over the danger's appearance." Governments qualify as racketeers under this definition *"to the extent that the threats against which a given government protects its citizens are imaginary or are consequences of its own activities"* — and *"since governments themselves commonly simulate, stimulate, or even fabricate threats of external war,"* much of modern state activity falls under the racketeer rather than the legitimate-protector heading.

## The Four-Part Model

Tilly's framework partitions state activity into four interdependent functions:

- **War making** — eliminating or neutralizing rivals outside the territory.
- **State making** — eliminating or neutralizing rivals inside the territory.
- **Protection** — eliminating or neutralizing the enemies of clients.
- **Extraction** — acquiring the means to carry out the first three.

Each activity feeds the next. War requires extraction; extraction builds fiscal-administrative machinery; state making suppresses internal competitors who could undermine extraction; protection binds favored classes to the regime. The mechanism is not a social contract; it is a historical sequence in which rulers' pursuit of war forced them into taxation, debt, conscription, courts, and administrative reach, and the resulting capacity persisted past the wars that produced it.

## Why the Distinction Matters

The "advantage of legitimacy" line is doing analytical work, not rhetorical work. Legitimacy, in the Stinchcombe formulation Tilly endorses, is *"the probability that other authorities will act to confirm the decisions of a given authority"*, not assent of the governed. A racketeer who can extract that confirmation — through monopoly of concentrated force in a delimited territory, recognition by other states, treaties, alliances — becomes a state, which is to say a "racketeer with sanctity." That is why state and racket sit on the same continuum rather than in opposed categories.
