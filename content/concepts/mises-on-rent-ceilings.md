# Mises on Rent Ceilings

> [[liberalism|Mises]] treats a residential-rent ceiling as the textbook instance of his general claim about effective price maxima. The mechanism is immediate, not contingent: at the ceiling, the housing market no longer clears, and the gap between the units demanded and the units offered shows up as a shortage. The Vienna passage names a regime that "virtually abolished residential rent" and registers the predicted outcome — a population that had *fallen* since the war combined with newly built municipal housing, and still "many thousands of persons are unable to find accommodations."

## The Passage

> "Wherever the government resorts to the fixing of prices, the result is always the same. When, for instance, the government fixes a ceiling on residential rents, a housing shortage immediately ensues. In Austria, the Social Democratic Party has virtually abolished residential rent. The consequence is that in the city of Vienna, for example, in spite of the fact that the population has declined considerably since the beginning of the World War and that several thousand new houses have been constructed by the municipality in the meantime, many thousands of persons are unable to find accommodations."

The framing the passage rejects is the assumption that a ceiling merely transfers rent from landlords to tenants. In [[liberalism|Liberalism]] Mises insists the ceiling alters the quantity supplied — not just who captures the rent — and that the resulting absence of the commodity "from the viewpoint" of the intervening authority "must appear as by far the greater evil." A subsequent paragraph supplies the dynamic: a regime unwilling to repeal the ceiling must escalate — rationing, ceilings on higher-order goods, wage controls, compulsory labor — until the choice is "either capitalism or socialism: there exists no middle way." The rent ceiling is therefore not an isolated welfare lever but the first step of an [[state-power-and-intervention|intervention-as-cumulative-process]] sequence.

## Mechanism

The price that a ceiling suppresses is the price that would have summarized the next-best use of the dwelling and the landlord's reservation. Under that price, units that did not clear at the ceiling are withdrawn from the market or shifted to other uses. The mechanism is the same one [[economic-calculation-problem|economic calculation]] names from the supply side and that [[knowledge-problem|the knowledge problem]] names from the information side. Repeal restores the price and with it the supply previously held off the market; the Mises Vienna passage is therefore directly testable, and the testing case-set has since included Buenos Aires 2023–2024.
